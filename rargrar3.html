About RARgRAR

History, mission, team, and operational guidelines for the RARgRAR Paranormal Entity Archive.

About This Site

Welcome, wanderer. You've found the dark corner. Pull up a chair — there is no leaving once you've read what we have to say.

RARgRAR was founded in October 2003 by a group of paranormal researchers, amateur folklorists, and people who had seen things they could not explain and desperately needed to tell someone. We began as a private forum, exchanging accounts via dial-up and early broadband connections. By 2005 we had documented over 200 individual entity encounters. In 2006 we went public. We have not slept soundly since.

The name comes from an account in our founding documents — a field note left by one of our earliest members after a night investigation in rural Ohio in 2002. At the bottom of three pages of meticulous documentation, after detailed descriptions of sounds and shapes and footprints in frozen mud, the notes simply end with two words written in increasingly shaky handwriting: RARgRAR. She never explained what she heard. She said explaining it would mean admitting it had a voice.

Operational Guidelines
  • Rule 1: Never post someone's real name without their explicit consent. The entities can hear you say names out loud. We're not entirely sure this doesn't apply to the internet.
  • Rule 2: All photographic evidence must include metadata. If it's been stripped, we note that. Stripped metadata means something wanted it gone.
  • Rule 3: Do not post from locations mentioned in active investigations. We had to add this rule in 2005. You can guess why.
  • Rule 4: If you are reading this website between 2:30am and 4:00am, we strongly encourage you to close your browser and turn on every light in your home. Then check that the lights in the next room came on too.
  • Rule 5: We are not responsible for what you find if you go looking. We document. We don't invite. The distinction matters to us. We're not sure it matters to them.
Awards & Recognition

RARgRAR was voted Best Horror Research Site by DarkWebAwards.net in 2005 and 2006. We were featured in a print paranormal magazine in 2004 whose title we have agreed not to share because the editor asked us very specifically not to and the way they asked suggested the request came from somewhere else entirely.

We were also cited in two university papers on digital folklore (both researchers requested anonymity after publication). We consider this a badge of honor. Or a warning label. We're still figuring out which.
